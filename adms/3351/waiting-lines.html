Waiting Lines

ADMS 3351 Operations Management

Two counters can serve customers at the same average rate and still have very different waits. Follow a customer through both lines, then work out why.

The coffee shop and the bank

Imagine two counters at a campus coffee shop. Both take four minutes per customer on average. At one counter, some orders are quick and others take longer. At the other, every order takes exactly four minutes. Which line would you join? In Operations Management, this is a waiting lines problem. Let's work through it using a bank teller and a self-service machine. Customers arrive randomly at the bank at an average rate of twelve per hour. Both the teller and the machine serve fifteen customers per hour on average. The teller’s service time varies. The machine takes exactly four minutes every time.

Follow customer B

Let’s follow one customer. Imagine they join each line at minute three. The machine finishes serving the previous customer at minute four, so they wait one minute. The teller finishes at minute six, so they wait three minutes. That’s one example of how a long transaction can hold up the line. Now let’s calculate the long-run averages.

Utilization

First, find utilization. That’s the fraction of time each server is busy. Divide twelve by fifteen to get zero point eight. Both servers are busy eighty percent of the time.

Calculate the average wait

For the teller, the average wait in line is called W sub Q. Start with the gap between the service rate and the arrival rate. Fifteen minus twelve is three. Multiply that gap by the service rate, fifteen. Then divide the arrival rate, twelve, by that product. The result is in hours. Multiply by sixty to get sixteen minutes. For the machine, the formula has an extra factor of two in the denominator. So divide sixteen by two. The average wait is eight minutes. In this example, keeping service time constant cuts the average wait in line in half.

Waiting versus total time

Time in line ends when service begins. Time in the system includes service. Add four minutes of average service time to each wait. That gives twenty minutes total for the teller and twelve minutes total for the machine.

How long is the total visit?

A customer waits 8 minutes before using the self-service machine. Service takes 4 minutes. How long is the customer in the system?

Check the answer

12 minutes. Time in the system includes both the 8-minute wait and the 4 minutes of service.
