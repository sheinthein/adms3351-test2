Process Capability

ADMS 3351 Operations Management

A car can be narrower than the garage door and still scrape the wall. Use a bakery’s cookie weights to see why capability takes two numbers, and what the gap between them tells you to fix.

The narrow garage

You are parking a car in a narrow garage. The car is narrower than the door, so it fits. But park too far to one side, and you still scrape the wall. Process capability works the same way, and it takes two numbers to see it.

The cookie specs

A bakery makes cookies for the campus café. Every cookie should weigh between 94 and 106 grams. Those are the lower and upper specification limits. The standard deviation is 1.5 grams, and right now the average cookie weighs 103 grams. Is this process capable? Pause, and decide.

Cp: compare the widths

Start with Cp. It compares the width of the specs to the width of the process. The specs are 12 grams wide. The process spread is 6 standard deviations, which is 9 grams. 12 divided by 9 is 1.33. That meets the usual target. The car is narrower than the garage.

Where is the centre?

But Cp never asks where the process is centred. The average is 103, not 100, so the heavy tail spills past the upper limit.

Cpk: check the centre

Cpk checks the centre. Measure from the average to each limit, then divide by three standard deviations, which is 4.5 grams. On the upper side, 106 minus 103 is 3. 3 divided by 4.5 is 0.67. On the lower side, the distance is 9. 9 divided by 4.5 is 2. Cpk is the smaller result: 0.67. The process is not capable.

What to fix

The gap between the two numbers tells you what to fix. Cp says the spread is fine. Cpk says the centre is off. So leave the oven alone. Move the average back to 100 grams, and Cpk rises to 1.33, the same as Cp.

What to remember

Cp asks, could it fit? Cpk asks, does it fit right now?

The idea to keep

Cp asks whether the process could fit. Cpk asks whether it fits right now.

When Cp is fine and Cpk is low, the spread is good and the mean is off-centre. Re-centre the process before you spend money reducing variation.

For your formula sheet

Capability, ignoring the centre
Cp = (USL − LSL) ÷ 6σ
Capability, at the current mean
Cpk = min[(USL − x̄) ÷ 3σ, (x̄ − LSL) ÷ 3σ]
Usual target
1.33 or higher
When centred
Cpk = Cp

Check your understanding

Answer one question, then move the process around yourself.

Capable or not?

A filling line has LSL = 20 mL, USL = 32 mL, a mean of 23 mL and a standard deviation of 1 mL. The target is 1.33. What do you conclude?

Show the worked answer

Cp = (32 − 20) ÷ (6 × 1) = 2.00. Cpk = min[(32 − 23) ÷ 3, (23 − 20) ÷ 3] = min(3.00, 1.00) = 1.00. The spread fits with room to spare, but the mean is too close to the lower limit, so the process misses the 1.33 target until it is re-centred near 26 mL.

Try it: move the process

The cookie limits stay at 94 g and 106 g. Shift the mean, tighten or loosen the spread, and see which number reacts.
